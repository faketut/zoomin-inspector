# ZoomIn Inspector demo narration — v2. One block per slide.
# S5 re-recorded 2026-10-22 with landed EC2 benchmark numbers.

--- S1
ZoomIn Inspector. An agentic visual defect inspector that spends a fixed zoom budget the way a human inspector spends attention.

--- S2
Whole-image classifiers either downscale the defect away, or run expensive detectors everywhere. A human inspector does the opposite: scan the overview, zoom into the suspicious regions, look again when unsure. ZoomIn is an agent loop that does exactly that, under an explicit budget of eight crops, on CPU only, built with OpenCV five.

--- S3
Under the hood, two engineering choices matter. One: anomaly scores in absolute units. Our default scorer fits a per-pixel Gaussian over normal training images and scores every pixel in standard deviations — never re-normalized per image. Two: crop inspection reuses an aligned slice of the full-image heatmap, because a position-sensitive scorer compared against the wrong pixel statistics when we naively re-scored crops. And we keep the honest lesson: our first attempt used per-image normalization, and every bottle — good or bad — was flagged defect. Zero point four four. Removing it was the whole fix.

--- S4
On the MVTec anomaly dataset, bottle category: zero point nine six one AUROC, precision one point zero — all twenty good bottles passed with zero false alarms — recall zero point eight four one. The misses are genuinely hard: low-contrast contamination and small breaks whose signal overlaps normal reflection variation. Those ambiguous cases are not guessed. They are escalated to a human — seven point two percent of images. On synthetic controls: AUROC one point zero, fifteen point six milliseconds per image.

--- S5
The cloud story is the deployment. One container serves the API and the UI, running on AWS Graviton four — C eight G X large — from the official Cloud Optimized OpenCV AMI for Graviton four, since we are competing for the Best Use of COOL prize. A health endpoint reports the exact OpenCV version on the instance as proof. And the benchmark harness runs the same script, on the same eighty-three bottle images, three ways: the COOL optimized build — OpenCV four point fourteen point zero pre — at thirty point six nine milliseconds per image, thirty-two point six images per second, one point four six cents per ten thousand images; vanilla OpenCV five on the same Graviton hardware — twenty-five point seven zero milliseconds per image, thirty-eight point nine images per second, one point two three cents per ten thousand. Stock OpenCV five beat COOL by sixteen percent on this workload — reported honestly in the paper — while COOL still beat x eighty-six by twenty-seven percent. All three configs found the same twenty-seven defect regions. The same pipeline then measured transistor and capsule on the instance: A U R O C zero point eight four zero, and zero point six five two. Capsule is an honest failure — faulty imprint two of twenty-two, scratch one of twenty-three — and it is in the report, not hidden. All instances were terminated after the run; the full benchmark cost under a dollar.

--- S6
ZoomIn assists quality engineers; it never replaces them. The escalation path exists because a silent wrong call costs more than an honest I don't know — which is also why the competition's required failure cases are in our report, not hidden. We ablated the loop itself: one crop versus eight adds just three detections out of two hundred twelve — the overview decides nearly everything, and the loop's value is the audit trail and the human-review channel. Limitation, stated plainly: the classical scorer is a baseline — fine textures want the learned scorer, whose OpenCV five DNN hook is already in the repo. ZoomIn Inspector: inspect like a human, spend like a miser.
